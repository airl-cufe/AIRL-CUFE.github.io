01 — THE INFRASTRUCTURE

Built to take your
research further.

From a first experiment to a demanding training run, shared infrastructure gives CUFE research teams a place to build, test, and learn.

01 / COMPUTE◈

GPU-powered research

Ten Intel i9 workstations with NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPUs, alongside three AMD Threadripper servers with NVIDIA RTX 5000 GPUs and 512 GB RAM each.

02 / SCHEDULING⌘

One managed cluster

A centralized Slurm workload manager schedules computing jobs across the lab. All compute-node work must go through the scheduler.

03 / STORAGE▤

Storage for each stage

Shared NFS storage separates home files, approved project data, and temporary scratch work—with different backup and cleanup policies for each.

04 / CONNECTIVITY⌁

Secure remote access

Approved users connect remotely over a two-hop SSH architecture: first the gateway, then the Slurm login node.

WHO IT'S FOR

Faculty & principal investigators · Researchers & postgraduate students · Senior students with graduation projects

04 — HOW TO USE THE LAB

Make your compute
count.

A practical first-run checklist, based on the lab's governance rules. Your account-specific connection details are provided after approval.

01

Connect in two hops

Use the credentials and hostnames issued to you: connect to the gateway server, authenticate, then connect to the Slurm login node. Never share passwords or SSH keys.

02

Prepare in your user space

Install research software in your own directory using Conda, virtual environments, containers, or other user-space installs. System-wide installs are reserved for lab administrators.

03

Submit compute work through Slurm

Submit computational jobs through the scheduler; don't run programs directly on compute nodes unless an administrator explicitly authorizes it. Request only the resources you need.

04

Check the limits before you submit

Review the quota table below. Interactive sessions are for debugging and small experiments: at most 2 hours, 1 GPU, and 16 GB RAM. Submit longer experiments as batch jobs.

05

Clean up and keep a backup

Home and project areas are backed up, but the lab does not guarantee permanent storage. Keep independent backups. Scratch is unbacked and files older than 7 days may be deleted.

BEFORE YOUR FIRST JOB

Keep this checklist handy.

  • Confirm your SSH details
  • Check your job and GPU quota
  • Choose the right storage area
  • Estimate dataset size before upload
  • Remove obsolete checkpoints

05 — GOVERNANCE & POLICIES

Shared resources.
Shared responsibility.

These essentials summarize the lab governance regulations. Approved users are responsible for reading and following the complete regulations.

◷

Use resources responsibly

Use lab resources for academic research, approved collaboration, and grant-funded projects. Request only the compute you need, monitor GPU utilization, and release resources when you're done. Idle GPU jobs may be terminated.

▦

Know where your data lives

Home is for code and documents (10 GB per user); project space is allocated per approved project; scratch is temporary, unbacked, and may be cleaned after 7 days. Datasets over 100 GB need administrator approval before upload.

⌑

Protect accounts & systems

Never share credentials or accounts. Don't attempt privilege escalation, scan internal networks, install malware, interfere with others' jobs, or circumvent security controls. Immediately report suspected credential compromise.

◉

Respect research & privacy

Your research data remains yours. Admins may access files only when needed for maintenance, security investigations, recovery, technical support, or institutional compliance. Operational and security activity may be monitored.

⊘

Keep use within policy

No cryptocurrency mining, unauthorized commercial services, illegal activity, copyright infringement, unapproved penetration testing, or excessive personal computing. Personal or unrelated data and backups don't belong on lab storage.

↗

Plan for continuity

Accounts are temporary and may expire at project completion, graduation, or after prolonged inactivity. Maintain independent data backups; the lab does not guarantee permanent storage. Maintenance can interrupt services.

Using the lab means agreeing to its current governance regulations and future updates.
